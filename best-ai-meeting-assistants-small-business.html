7 Best AI Meeting Assistants for Small Businesses in 2027

Compare tools that can record or capture meeting audio, turn conversations into notes, help find important details and make follow-up easier.

Updated: November 2026 · By BizTech Compass

Meetings can create a lot of work after everyone leaves the call. Someone has to remember decisions, write up notes, send follow-ups and track action items. An AI meeting assistant can help by transcribing conversations, summarising key points and making information easier to revisit.

But not every business needs the same kind of assistant. Some tools are built around searchable meeting records and team collaboration, while others focus on personal note-taking or live transcription. This guide compares seven options by their practical strengths and trade-offs. We have not independently tested every product, so product descriptions are research-based and should not be mistaken for hands-on reviews.

What is an AI meeting assistant?

An AI meeting assistant is software that helps capture and process the content of meetings. Depending on the product, it may record or transcribe conversations, identify speakers, create summaries, highlight decisions, suggest action items and let you search or ask questions about past meeting content.

These tools can reduce the need to write everything down while talking, but their output is not a guaranteed accurate record. Names, figures, decisions and action items should be checked before sharing or relying on the notes.

1. Fireflies.ai — for searchable meeting records

Fireflies.ai is an AI meeting assistant that captures conversations, creates transcripts and summaries, and helps organise meeting information so it can be searched and revisited. Its AI features can help users query information from their meeting records and turn conversations into follow-up work.

Best for: consultants, small teams and client-facing businesses that hold recurring meetings and need to find details later.

Practical use: A consultant could keep client discovery calls in a searchable archive, then look back for a requirement, decision or unresolved question when preparing a proposal.

What to consider: A full meeting archive can be valuable, but it may be more than someone who only has occasional calls needs. Check integrations, recording options, storage limits and privacy controls before adopting it.

Pricing considerations: Review how the free plan limits recording, transcription, storage and AI features, and confirm what is included in paid tiers.

Our take: Fireflies is worth considering when meetings are an ongoing source of business information—not simply something you need a quick summary of. If you mostly want a straightforward transcript, compare it with Otter.ai; if you prefer an easy summary-first experience, also look at Fathom.

2. Otter.ai — for transcription and live notes

Otter.ai converts speech into text and provides meeting notes, summaries, speaker identification and tools for reviewing or searching conversations. It is designed to help people follow a discussion without having to manually capture every detail.

Best for: freelancers, consultants, interviewers and teams who place a high value on transcripts and searchable meeting notes.

Practical use: After a client briefing, you can review the transcript to check the exact wording of a request, revisit details or pull out next steps before sending a follow-up message.

What to consider: Transcription accuracy can be affected by accents, overlapping speakers, audio quality, technical terms and background noise. Usage allowances and advanced features may be restricted by plan.

Pricing considerations: If you have frequent or long meetings, compare transcription allowances, file imports, export options and collaboration features across plans.

Our take: Otter makes sense if a transcript is central to your workflow. If you mainly want an easy meeting summary and don't need to work with transcripts as often, Fathom is another option to consider.

3. Fathom — for quick summaries and follow-ups

Fathom is an AI meeting assistant that records supported meetings and produces summaries, transcripts and highlights to help users review conversations and identify follow-up items.

Best for: small business owners and teams who want to get the gist of a meeting and revisit important moments without manually writing extensive notes.

Practical use: A business owner finishing several calls in a day could use summaries to refresh their memory, check decisions and prepare follow-up emails or task lists.

What to consider: Confirm which features and integrations are included in the plan you choose. As with any automated notes, review the summary for missing context or incorrectly attributed decisions.

Pricing considerations: Check the current free offering, limits on AI features and what paid plans add for individual or team use.

Our take: Fathom is a useful candidate when your main goal is to make meetings easier to review. If you want a more searchable, organised record across many conversations, compare it with Fireflies.ai.

4. tl;dv — for highlights and team meeting review

tl;dv is a meeting recording and AI note-taking platform that helps teams capture discussions, generate summaries, create highlights and share useful moments from calls.

Best for: teams reviewing sales calls, customer conversations, interviews or internal meetings when not everyone needs to attend every session.

Practical use: A team member could share a short clip of a customer explaining a problem rather than forwarding an entire meeting recording, helping colleagues understand the context.

What to consider: Recording, sharing and collaboration features may introduce more setup and administration than an individual note-taking app. Check access permissions, plan limits and your team's consent process.

Pricing considerations: Compare limits for recordings, AI summaries, clips, integrations and team collaboration before choosing a plan.

Our take: tl;dv is more compelling when meeting content needs to be shared and reviewed across a team. If you mainly take personal notes and want to avoid a meeting bot, consider Granola.

5. MeetGeek — for structured meeting workflows

MeetGeek captures meeting information and helps turn conversations into transcripts, summaries, key points and action items. It also offers features aimed at organising meeting information and connecting it with other work tools.

Best for: small businesses with regular meetings that want a consistent way to create notes and carry information into follow-up workflows.

Practical use: A small agency could use meeting summaries as a starting point for project updates, handover notes or a list of tasks to check after client calls.

What to consider: Make sure its integrations and templates fit the way you actually work. More automation does not automatically mean a better workflow—review and correct the output before it is shared or acted on.

Pricing considerations: Look closely at recording and transcription allowances, storage, AI features and integration availability at each plan level.

Our take: MeetGeek may suit businesses looking to make meeting notes part of a repeatable process. If your needs are simpler and more personal, Granola may be a better fit.

6. Granola — for bot-free personal meeting notes

Granola is an AI meeting notepad built around your own notes. It captures meeting audio through your device and enhances notes with AI, rather than relying on a visible meeting bot joining the call. Users can work with notes, templates and AI-assisted summaries.

Best for: freelancers, consultants and individuals who prefer a personal note-taking experience without a meeting bot participant.

Practical use: During a project discussion, you can jot down key points or questions, then use the AI-enhanced notes to help organise the conversation into a clearer record and next steps.

What to consider: Bot-free capture does not mean recording is invisible, consent is unnecessary or privacy concerns disappear. Check how audio is captured, what is stored, how data is processed and whether its team-sharing capabilities meet your needs.

Pricing considerations: Review note limits, AI usage, collaboration features and paid plan differences if you use it frequently.

Our take: Granola is an appealing alternative for people who primarily need their own organised notes. If you need shared recordings, clips and team review workflows, compare it with tl;dv.

7. Read AI — for meeting reports and insights

Read AI provides meeting summaries, transcripts and reports, with additional features designed to surface insights from conversations. Depending on the available plan and product settings, it may include analytics and ways to review recurring meeting themes or participation patterns.

Best for: managers and teams who want more than a transcript and are interested in structured reports or meeting trends.

Practical use: A manager might review meeting summaries and recurring topics to spot follow-up questions or communication issues that deserve attention—then validate those observations with context and direct feedback.

What to consider: Analytics such as talk time or participation indicators are only signals, not definitive measures of performance, contribution or meeting quality. Use them thoughtfully and transparently.

Pricing considerations: Check current report limits, analytics, integrations, admin options and the features available in each tier.

Our take: Read AI is worth exploring if reports and insights are important to your meeting process. If your priority is straightforward transcription and reviewing what was said, Otter.ai may be a simpler starting point.

How to choose the right AI meeting assistant

If you're a freelancer or consultant

Start with the task that creates the most friction: capturing client requirements, remembering commitments or preparing follow-up messages. Otter.ai, Fathom or Granola could be reasonable starting points depending on whether you prefer transcripts, quick summaries or personal notes.

If you're running a small team

Look for reliable shared access, searchable records, permissions, integrations and consistent summaries. Fireflies.ai or MeetGeek may be worth comparing for an ongoing meeting workflow; tl;dv is worth considering when sharing clips and reviewing calls are important.

If you want searchable transcripts

Prioritise transcript accuracy, speaker labels, search, export and audio playback. Compare Otter.ai with Fireflies.ai, and test them with the type of meetings you actually have.

If you want to avoid a meeting bot

Explore bot-free capture options such as Granola, but check the recording method and communicate clearly with meeting participants. A bot-free workflow is not a substitute for consent or privacy checks.

If analytics matter

Review Read AI and any other candidate's reporting features, but use metrics carefully. Numbers can miss context and should not be treated as a standalone assessment of employees or customers.

If your budget is limited

Try a free plan or trial before paying, where available. Check how quickly you might reach recording, transcription, storage or AI limits. Avoid paying for team or analytics features unless you have a clear use for them.

Are AI meeting assistants worth it for small businesses?

They can be useful if meetings contain information you regularly need to revisit, if writing notes distracts you from the discussion, or if follow-up tasks are often forgotten. They may be less valuable if you have few meetings, already maintain concise notes or spend more time correcting automated summaries than you save.

Try one tool on a low-risk meeting type first. Check the notes against the original conversation, measure whether it genuinely reduces your follow-up workload, and only then consider using it more widely.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI meeting assistant for a small business?

It depends on your workflow. Fireflies.ai is worth looking at for searchable meeting records, Otter.ai for transcription-centric notes, Fathom for summary-led review, tl;dv for team highlights, MeetGeek for structured workflows, Granola for personal bot-free notes and Read AI for reports and insights. Compare the features you need rather than choosing by brand alone.

Can AI meeting assistants automatically create action items?

Many offer action-item extraction or follow-up suggestions. Treat these as drafts: confirm the task, owner and deadline before adding them to your official workflow.

Are AI meeting assistants free?

Several providers offer free tiers or trials, but limits and included features vary and can change. Review the current official plan details before signing up.

Do AI meeting assistants need to join my video call?

Some services use a meeting bot that joins the call, while other tools offer different capture approaches. Check how the specific product works, and let participants know when audio is being recorded or transcribed.

Are AI meeting summaries accurate?

They can be helpful but are not guaranteed to be accurate or complete. Verify decisions, names, numbers, deadlines and action items before sharing the notes or relying on them.

Is a bot-free meeting notepad automatically more private?

No. The recording method is only one consideration. Understand audio capture, data processing, storage, retention, access controls and consent obligations for the tool you use.

Final verdict

The right AI meeting assistant is the one that makes your real meeting workflow easier—not the one with the longest feature list. If you need to revisit conversations, start by comparing transcription, summaries, search and sharing. If you prefer personal notes, evaluate a bot-free approach. If you manage a team, consider collaboration and administration alongside the AI output.
